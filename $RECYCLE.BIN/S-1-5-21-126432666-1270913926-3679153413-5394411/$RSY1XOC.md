    - **1.2.1** Perimeter security mechanisms (e.g., cameras, alarms, security guards, etc.) must be implemented, as

  - **1.3** Access controls must be implemented to restrict physical access.
    - **1.3.1** Physical access control mechanisms (e.g., identification badges, controlled reception, cameras, access
logging, etc.) must be implemented at office buildings, data centers, and other locations.
    - **1.3.2** Access to physical locations must be restricted only to Siemens personnel who require access.
    - **1.3.3** Visitor access control mechanisms (e.g., visitor logs, escort requirements, etc.) must be defined and
implemented commensurate with the sensitivity of the physical location.
    - **1.3.4** At a minimum, Siemens staff must supervise the maintenance activities of maintenance personnel
without required access authorization and escort guests.
    - **1.3.5** Physical keys that are used to secure rooms, datacenters, or similar areas must be inventoried and a
periodic review of those physical keys must be conducted.
    - **1.3.6** Physical keys used to secure cabinets and drawers must be inventoried.
    - **1.3.7** Employees must report lost, stolen, or damaged keys to the appropriate manager in a timely manner.
    - **1.3.8** Reviews of access to sensitive physical locations must be performed periodically.
    - **1.3.9** The organization must define the requirements for the removal of physical assets, including when the
removal must be logged. When the removal is logged, it must include the type of asset being removed,
individual that is responsible for the asset upon removal, and the approving authority.
    - **1.3.10** Access must be revoked upon termination or change of employment.
    - **1.3.11** Delivery and loading areas must be segregated from internal secure areas.
  - **1.4** Environmental protection controls must be implemented to help ensure physical locations are protected
against external and environmental threats.
    - **1.4.1** Environmental control mechanisms must be implemented at physical locations. Environmental control
mechanisms may include heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC), water drainage, fire suppression,
emergency lighting, power distribution unit (PDU) implementation, continuous power, humidity control, etc.
    - **1.4.2** Access to alter environmental control mechanisms must be restricted to authorized personnel.
    - **1.4.3** Cables within the office, storage IT closets, and datacenters shall be protected from damage or
interference with considerations for environmental impact which may include but is not limited to flooding.
    - **1.4.4** Equipment maintenance should follow a defined service schedule. Prior to scheduled maintenance,
where possible, any confidential information should be cleared from the equipment.
  - **1.5** Monitoring of physical security and environmental-related incidents must be implemented.
    - **1.5.1** The effectiveness of perimeter security and access control mechanisms, as well as environmental
controls, must be continuously monitored and periodically evaluated.
    - **1.5.2** Data from access control and video surveillance mechanisms (e.g., access logs, video recordings, etc.)
must be retained in accordance with defined retention schedules.
    - **1.5.3** Physical and environmental security incidents must be monitored and responded to in accordance
with an established incident management policy and incident response plans.
  - **1.6** Remote work sites (such as working from home) must have appropriate physical protection mechanisms in
place and meet the requirements of the SISW Company Directives based on the classification of data accessed or
stored by an employee in that remote work site.
SISW Saas Secure Development Policy requirements
  - **1.1** Security must be incorporated into all phases of the software and services development lifecycle.
    - **1.1.1** Secure software and services development processes must be established and implemented to
facilitate the assessment of security risks and requirements during development.
      - **1.1.1.1** Secure development processes must include risk modeling, such as threat modeling, attack
modeling, or attack surface mapping to help assess the security risk for the software
      - **1.1.1.2** Where possible, secure development processes must be defined to ensure that the necessary
information to support security evaluation criteria are effectively gathered. Automation is
recommended.
