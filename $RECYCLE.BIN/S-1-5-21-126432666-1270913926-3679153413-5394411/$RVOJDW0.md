SISW Saas Access Control Policy requirements
1.1. Processes for assignment of access rights, including privileged access rights to information resources must be
established.
1.1.1. The process and its documentation for assigning access rights must be defined, documented, and
reviewed annually and/or when there are changes to the process.
1.1.2. A formal user registration and deregistration procedure must be established, using unique user IDs
that make it possible for users to be linked to, and made responsible for, their actions.
1.1.3. Records of account creation and access approval must be retained for all accounts.
1.1.4. User access to information resources must be assigned on a need-to-know basis.
1.1.5. Default user privileges and administrative access must be granted based on the requirements of their
job or function.
1.1.6. Service accounts and shared user accounts shall have an assigned person who holds overall
responsibility and a record of users who are authorized to use the account.
1.1.7. An approval must be obtained from the information resource owner/admin and documented prior to
granting access; moreover, this approval process must account for user credentials as well as service or non
interactive credentials.
1.1.8. Information resource owner must establish access criteria based on asset criticality, per the Data
Classification, Handling, and Sharing Policy, and considering SISW's Information Security Policy and information
security objectives.
1.1.9. Use of system utilities and secrets must be limited to the minimum practical number of trusted,
authorized users.
1.1.10. Any access to information resources or assets should implement security measures when personnel
gaining such access are working remotely for the purposes of protecting information accessed, processed, or
stored outside SISW's premises.
1.1.11. Where possible, data masking techniques must be considered to protect access to sensitive
information.
1.2. Assignment of access rights, including privileged access rights to in-scope ISMS applications, systems, data,
related infrastructure and supporting tools is based on authorization.
1.2.1. The process for assigning access rights to applications, systems, data, related infrastructure and
supporting tools is defined, documented, and reviewed annually and/or when there are changes to the
process.
1.2.2. An approval must be obtained and documented prior to granting access to applications, systems, data,
related infrastructure and supporting tools.
1.2.3. Upon termination, application, systems, data, related infrastructure and supporting tool access must
be disabled.
1.2.4. Management must perform a review of access rights to sensitive applications, systems, data, related
infrastructure and supporting tools to ensure access appropriateness on a periodic basis.

1.3. Authentication mechanisms are implemented and enforced.
1.3.1. Authentication mechanisms such as username/password, certificate, or token must be required to
validate a user's, process's, or device's identity before granting access to SISW Saas information resources.
1.3.2. Unique user IDs must be established for each user.
1.3.3. IDs, regardless of type, must make it possible for users, devices, or processes acting on behalf of users
or devices, to be linked to, and made responsible for, their actions.
1.3.4. Unique identifiers must be disabled after a defined period of inactivity, as defined by the team
implementing the system.
1.3.5. System identifiers must be disabled when removed from service.
1.3.6. Unique user IDs cannot be re-used for a certain number of days after they are disabled, as defined by
the implementing team, and until the identifier refers to a unique entity.
1.3.7. Password requirements must be established for access to SISW Saas information resources, including
requirements related to complexity, lockout, and password reset.
1.3.8. Multi-factor authentication must be implemented based upon the criticality of SISW Saas information
resources.
1.3.8.1. MFA is mandatory for privileged accounts and remote access.
1.3.9. Unsuccessful logon attempts must be limited.
1.3.10. Sessions must be locked or terminated after a period of inactivity and after any other defined
termination conditions.
1.3.10.1. Conditions must be determined for ending sessions of access, including terminating the
session and an inactivity lockout timer.
1.3.11. Accounts inactive for a maximum period of 180 days must be disabled after verification unless
specified and approved otherwise.
1.3.12. A user notification must be displayed for all systems.
1.3.13. Authentication feedback must be obscured during authentication.
1.3.14. Remote access must only be permissible through VPN or other methods approved prior to
authentication.
1.3.15. Replay resistance mechanisms must be in place for authentication with any systems or applications.
1.4 Access to information resources must be reviewed on a periodic basis.
1.4.1. Review of access rights must be documented and performed at least at an interval of every 6 months
by management or authorized representative to ensure that all initial and continual access provisioned to
information resources, assets, and physical locations is appropriate and based on an approved authorization.
1.5 Modification of access rights, including privileged access rights to information resources is based on changes to
employment status (e.g. role changes, transfers).
1.5.1. The process for disabling of inactive or unauthorized accounts must be defined, documented and
logged.
1.5.2. Upon termination, access must be disabled within one business day unless formally specified and
approved differently.
1.5.3. Procedures must be established for access modification (upon role or privilege change) within a
defined time period.
1.5.4. Access modifications must be performed appropriately upon transfer or change of roles and
responsibilities.
1.5.5. All credentials, such as passwords, tokens, and keys, shall be changed, disabled, or deleted upon
termination or transfer, including accounts out of SISW's control, such as a web portal from a third-party
supplier.

1.1 Processes must be implemented, and requirements must be established for information security and the
continuity of information security management in adverse situations.
1.1.1. Documented plans, response and continuity requirements must be developed and approved,
detailing how SISW Saas will manage a disruptive event and will maintain its information security to a

predetermined level, based on information security continuity objectives.
1.1.2. Key individuals must be identified, and an adequate management structure must be in place to
prepare for, mitigate and respond to a disruptive event using personnel with the necessary authority,
experience and competence.
1.1.3. Responsibility must be assigned for providing guidance for Business Continuity Plan (BCP)
development, maintenance, and testing and for providing and maintaining a solution for centralized plan
retention.
1.1.4. Business impact analysis (BIA) shall be performed to identify critical systems assets, and information
communication technology (ICT) to determine continuity requirements, RTO (recovery time objectives) and
RPO (recovery point objectives) where expectations and strategies are established for all disruption stages.
1.1.5. Notifications of any material changes must be sent to all applicable functions and partners.
1.1.6. Functional units to whom this policy applies must be briefed at least annually to ensure their
understanding of BCP requirements.
1.2 Processes, requirements, and controls must be documented, implemented, and maintained to ensure the
required level of continuity for information security during an adverse situation.
1.2.1. A unified framework for BCP must be maintained to ensure the consistency and accuracy of the
plans/process between functions.
1.2.2. Critical business functions must be identified and analyzed for the impact of their loss or interruption.
1.2.3. Priorities for recovery of specific functions and support systems must be determined.
1.2.4. BCP capabilities must be adequately resourced to deliver key planning, maintenance, exercising, and
reporting activities.
1.2.5. Backup and restoration measures for information, systems and services must be established and
regularly tested.
1.3 Requirements must be implemented to ensure information security continuity controls are verified at regular
intervals to assess their validity and effectiveness during adverse situations.
1.3.1. BCP/DR must be exercised at least once every 12 months or when significant changes occur. The tests
may include planned outages, live exercises or walkthroughs/tabletop discussions.
1.3.2. BCP/DR test cases must be documented. Expected outcomes (e.g. a runbook) must be included in the
documentation.
1.3.3. The results of the execution of BCP/DR test cases including who participated in the testing exercise,
details of the results obtained and any unexpected outcomes must be retained.
1.3.4. Deficiencies discovered during testing must be remediated through the change management process.
1.3.5. Planned testing must take place within a scheduled timeframe to avoid any disruption to SISW Saas
information resources.
1.3.6. The BCP must be evaluated for reasonability against contractual, legal, environmental, and business
requirements.
1.4 Administrative and technical controls must be implemented at information processing and corporate facilities
with redundancy commensurate to meet availability requirements.
1.5 SISW must implement capacity monitoring mechanisms for information resources to ensure the appropriate
level of capacity is met.
1.5.1. SISW must consider reviewing capacity requirements periodically taking into account projections of
future capacity requirements and trends.
1.5.2. Where possible, SISW should conduct stress tests on all systems and services to ensure the
appropriate level of capacity is available to meet peak performance requirements.
1.5.3. For any critical systems identified by SISW, plans regarding capacity management must be
documented.

SISW Saas Cryptography Policy requirements
1.1 Encryption methods should be leveraged to protect data at rest, or data in-transit when possible.
1.2 Data at rest should be encrypted using symmetric key encryption methods such as AES, with key strength set to
a minimum 128-bit length to ensure best protection.
1.2.1. Hardware based technologies should be leveraged where possible, and in conjunction with any
software/OS based encryption as a second layer of protection for data at rest.
1.2.2. Where possible, software based encryption should be enabled by default on both fixed media and
removable media, where hardware based encryption might not be feasible.
1.2.3. Encryption keys and passwords associated with symmetric key encryption should not be stored in
plain text format. Password vaults should be leveraged, with full audit trail functionality to capture
access/modification activity at a user level.
1.3 Data in-transit should leverage end to end encryption, and encryption at multiple layers (Application, Transport,
Network etc.) should be enabled to offer maximum protection.
1.3.1. Asymmetric encryption, when in service, should leverage a minimum key strength of 2048 bits, and
must use a strong hashing algorithm such as SHA-2 256 or greater.
1.3.2. Secret keys shall not be available in plain text and shall be stored and transmitted encrypted using a
strong password or wrapping key technique.
1.3.2.1. Secure Key Management Systems (KMS) shall be used to store secret keys in the cloud
1.3.3. SSL/TLS certificates should be rotated on a regular basis, and a certificate used for public facing
services should not be used for more than a 2-year period.
1.3.3.1. TLS protocol version 1.2 or higher must be used.
1.3.3.2. The use of SSL 1.0, SSL 2.0, SSL 3.0, TLS 1.0, and TLS 1.1 is not allowed.
1.3.4. Wild card certificates will not be used on any Cloud Accounts and Public Facing Services and should
be used sparingly for private/internal services to encourage best practices.
1.3.5. Self-signed certificates may only be used with prior review and approval by DI SW Cybersecurity
Officer

1.3.6. Certificates from only the Siemens Server Registration Authority (ServerRA) AWS, QuoVadis, Digicert
or Let's Encrypt shall be used for Cloud Webservers accessible from the internet.
1.3.7. Certificate providers following the Web Trust Principles and Criteria for Certification Authorities may

1.3.8. In addition to key based security, the organization will also review strength of cipher suites

1.4 All private communication tunnels (such as VPN) established between production network and remote
networks should leverage IPsec, or an industry standard equivalent.
1.4.1. Individual IPsec tunnels should leverage independent pre-shared keys, and keys should not be reused.
1.4.2. IPsec pre-shared keys should not be stored in plain text format. Password vaults should be leveraged,
with full audit trail functionality to capture access/modification activity at a user level.
1.5. Generation of keys, tokens, passphrases shall follow the applicable vendor or service recommended best
practices, or shall conform to the requirements below absent any recommendations
1.5.1. Each instance of key, token or certificate shall be generated using a unique input or seeding value;
where possible a random seeding key shall be applied using a random number/key generator application or
program
1.5.1.1. Where applicable, the random seeding key shall be deleted irretrievably. Where a
recovery process is necessary and the seeding key should be preserved, a secured keystore or
vault shall be used for the purposes of storing the key and access controls applied, conforming to
the SISW Saas Access Control policy.
1.5.1.2. The storage of seeding keys in unencrypted formats on endpoints is strictly prohibited,
and the storage of seeding keys as images of QR codes is also strictly prohibited.
1.5.2. Where key strength, cipher and hashing techniques for the purposes of generation of private and
public keys is customizable, industry best practices and recommendations shall be followed.
1.5.3. When generating keys, if an expiration attribute is customizable, the lifetime of such secrets/keys shall
follow the policies within table 1 of this document
1.6. Storage of private keys, tokens and passphrases shall be secured, vaulted using access control mechanisms as
recommended within the SISW Saas Access Control Policy
1.6.1. Where practicable, SISW owned and operated key vaults shall be leveraged for purposes of storing
secrets
1.6.2. A secure vault or KMS shall be the primary storage mechanism for keys, tokens and passphrases, and
the storage of such keys on any endpoints, scripts, code or other human readable format is strictly prohibited.
1.6.2.1. If necessary to host a secret/private key on an endpoint, applicable security mechanisms
from within this policy shall be enforced on such systems. For example, storage of private keys on
a server shall be possible only if the private key file is encrypted via a secret, and disk encryption
has been applied.
1.6.2.2. Where necessary, a key encrypting key or wrapping key shall be applied for the
protection of highly critical secrets.
1.6.3. Access to the storage location shall be moderated by SISW approved identity and access management
services, and a full audit trail shall be available to ensure both ad min and user actions on such secrets are
recorded.
1.6.4. The copying of secrets out of vaults shall be technically restricted where possible. Logging and
monitoring for such efforts shall be applied in accordance with the SISW Saas Logging and Monitoring policy
1.6.5. Access reviews of the key storage vaults shall be performed regularly, in accordance with the SISW
Saas Access Control Policy.
1.7. Where possible, the use of static certificates, keys, tokens and passphrases as authentication/authorization
credentials should be a secondary mechanism, and not deployed as the primary identification factor
1.8. Static Keys, token, passphrases shall be securely disposed at the conclusion of the use case or end of life, and
shall not be reused
1.8.1. Keys, tokens and passphrases stored in key vaults shall be deleted/removed using applicable vault
mechanisms.
1.8.2. Key attributes and seeding keys used to generate secrets shall also be disposed when the key/secret is
disposed.

SISW Saas Data Backup and Recovery Policy requirements
1.1 Siemens has assigned levels for data classification, and all data backed up must be subject to same classification
level as was the data source.
1.1.1. Data backed up will be handled as per the requirements associated with the data classification level,
as detailed in the Siemens Industry Software (SISW) Saas Data Classification, Handling and Sharing Policy.
1.2 Data backup methodology used must preserve data structure as found in the source, with clear labeling of
source of data, and location hierarchy as found in the source.
1.2.1. Data scope for the backup must be defined by considering the respective environment which can
include configuration data, license files, and contractual requirements
1.3 Data backup schedules must leverage incremental, daily and weekly/monthly rollup routines to optimize
recovery point objective (RPO) and storage efficiencies.
1.4 Data backed up should be stored in a location isolated from the source, minimally at a logical level.
1.5 Data backup routines must generate an activity log, and the log information should be reviewed on a regular
basis to verify job status. Backup logs are subject to policies set forth in the Siemens Industry Software (SISW) Saas
Secure Logging and Monitoring Policy.
1.6 Data backed up must be replicated to an offsite location or alternate Cloud availability zone based on data
criticality and classification. Offsite replication must also be logged and reviewed to ensure job completion.
1.7 Data backed up must be tested via restore tests on a regular basis. The tests must be conducted to verify data
availability and integrity to ensure any recovery point objective (RPO) and recovery time objective (RTO)
requirements.
1.1.1. Data restore activity must be logged, subject to policies set forth in the Siemens Industry Software
(SISW) Saas Secure Logging and Monitoring Policy.
1.1.2. Data restore activity must include data backup restore tests that are inclusive of information,
software, and systems.
SISW Saas Data Classification, Handling, and Sharing Policy requirements
1.1 Data classification levels must be documented and implemented based on established criteria.
1.1.1. Data classification levels must be defined in consideration of the following impacts, should the data
be inappropriately disclosed:
• Regulatory, legal, and contractual liabilities
• Financial liabilities
• Operational impacts
• Damages to reputation or public image
• Potential physical harms
1.1.2. Data sets containing data from multiple classification levels must be classified at the level of the most
sensitive data type within the data set.
1.1.3. SISW Saas data must be classified according to the Siemens Data Classification Acceptable Use
Document, which allocates the following classification levels.
Strictly Confidential: This applies to corporate proprietary information, for which unwanted disclosure
can have "major" risk impact e.g. for an Operating Company (see ERM scale). This is information that is


---

## Page 7

crucial for the success and survival of the entire company or parts of it. Disclosure can cause very serious
harm to business interests and goals of the company, lead to grave legal consequences, impinge on the
value of Siemens stock or severely damage the company's reputation (e.g. company or sales strategy
papers, economic and budget plans, information on merger and acquisition topics). This information
should be distributed on a need-to-know basis and requires a non-disclosure agreement for sharing with
people outside the company.
Confidential: This applies to corporate proprietary information, for which unwanted disclosure can have
"moderate" or "significant" risk impact e.g. for an Operating Company (see ERM scale). The information
can be valuable for competitors (e.g. controlling data, procurement and sales contracts, source code of
software developed by SISW Saas or design documents). This information should be distributed on a
need-to-know basis and requires a non-disclosure agreement for sharing with people outside the
company.
Restricted: This applies to corporate proprietary information, for which unwanted disclosure can have
only "marginal" or "minor" risk impact for e.g. an Operating Company (see ERM scale). This information
may be accessible for a large circle of employees on need-to-know basis, but not generally available to
people outside the company (e.g. organizational plans, internal telephone directories, internal
guidelines, test reports, etc.). This information may be distributed externally on a need-to-know basis
after the external parties are informed about their obligations. Consider a non-disclosure agreement
when sharing with people outside the company.
Unrestricted: This kind of classification applies to corporate information, which is intended for public
use or where disclosure has no negative impact to Siemens (e.g. press releases, job postings, official
price lists, advertising materials, etc.). It is either labelled as "unrestricted" or immediately identifiable as
for public-use information (e.g. an internet web site).
1.1.4. All data stored on the cloud is classified as Confidential by definition. As such, there is no need for
tagging or other marking.
1.1.5. All source code and software design documentation is classified as Confidential by definition. As
such, when these artifacts are stored within a controlled system (e.g. Gitlab, Polarion, Jira, etc.) there is no
need for further tagging or other marking.
1.1.6. All Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) referenced in NIST 800-171 is classified as Confidential
by definition.
1.2 Data handling and sharing requirements must be documented and implemented based on established criteria.
1.2.1. Data handling requirements must be defined for:
1.2.1.1. Data stored within SISW Saas information resources (e.g., databases, servers,
workstations, mobile devices, etc.) and environments of Third-Party Providers
1.2.1.2. The classification of an asset shall follow the classification of the most sensitive
classified data within that asset
1.2.1.3. Data transmitted across production, corporate, and public networks
1.2.2. Requirements must be established for sharing data with authorized internal and external entities.
1.2.3. SISW Saas data must be handled and shared according to the Siemens AG Cybersecurity Card -
Protection of Sensitive Data.
1.2.4. Users shall either manually activate the "lock screen" or logout before leaving a system unattended.
1.2.5. A non-disclosure agreement must be executed in accordance with the rules defined in 1.1.3 when
sharing information outside the company.
1.2.6. The requirements for data and resource handling, according to their security classification and
Acceptable Use within the SISW Company Directives, apply regardless of location.
1.2.7. Mechanisms shall be implemented to conceal sensitive information such as PII by means of, including
but not limited to, data masking, pseudonymization, and anonymization in accordance with the established
classification criteria.
1.2.8. Measures regarding data leakage prevention shall be implemented across all data classified as Strictly
Confidential, Confidential and Restricted.
1.2.9. Data retention periods shall be established and documented with consideration to legal and
regulatory requirements in accordance with the established classification criteria.

SISW Saas Incident Management Policy requirements
1.1 Information security threat intelligence data must be continually obtained and evaluated.
1.1.1. Contacts with authorities, external interest groups, or forums that handle issues related to incidents
must be maintained.
1.1.2. SISW must determine internal resources that are assigned responsibilities with regards to receiving,
analyzing, and prioritizing the response to security threats and risks to the organization.
1.2 Processes for SISW personnel and external entities to report incidents must be established. *Please refer to
the policy document to view the Incident Response Steps flowchart.*
1.2.1. Employees/contractors must immediately report all potential incidents.
1.2.2. SISW CISO and/or DI IT ISO shall be informed and involved based on the severity of the security
incident.
1.2.3. Potential incidents must be recorded by applying the corresponding tool.
1.2.4. The validity of reported incidents must be evaluated according to established incident classification
criteria.
1.2.5. In the event of a potential incident involving PII (Personally Identifiable Information), the SISW Data
Privacy Officer shall be involved.
1.3 An incident response and remediation plan must be established, implemented, reviewed, and tested.
1.3.1. An incident response plan must be established to formalize processes for classifying, escalating,
detecting, analyzing, containing, eradicating, recovering from, and reporting incidents.
1.3.2. A communications model must be established to ensure the timely exchange of information related
to incidents.
1.3.3. Response activities and observations must be documented in accordance with established criteria
based on incident severity ratings.
1.3.4. Procedures for handling forensic evidence must be established and account for:
• Chain of custody
• Integrity and storage of evidence
1.3.5. Post-mortems of critical and high severity incidents as well as events that were not initially classified
as incidents (i.e., false negatives) must be performed.
1.3.6. The incident response plan must be reviewed and tested at least annually.
1.4 A remediation plan must be executed before incident closure.
1.4.1. The remediation steps must be documented.
1.4.2. The incident closure must be communicated.
1.4.3. Lessons learned must be executed with the intention to identify and amend incident process gaps and
eliminate incident causes.
1.5 Core and extended members of the Computer Security Incident Response Team must be trained upon
onboarding and periodically thereafter.
1.5.1. The incident response plan must be shared with personnel as a component of the Incident Response
team onboarding process.
1.5.2. Periodic trainings (e.g., tabletop exercises) must be performed to refresh the Incident Response team
members' knowledge of incident response processes and responsibilities.

SISW Saas Information Security Risk Management Policy requirements
1.1. Information Security Risk Assessment
1.1.1. Information security management identifies control and risk owners with insight into and governance
over risk areas.
1.1.2. Information security management, in conjunction with control and risk owners, develops a security
risk assessment process that can be applied consistently, valid, and comparable results.
1.1.3. The risk assessment process includes an assessment of inherent risks, including the likelihood and
impact for a loss of confidentiality, integrity, availability and privacy of information and systems to the business.
1.1.4. The risk assessment process results in the identification of information security risks to organizational
operations (including mission, functions, image, or reputation), organizational assets, and individuals, resulting
from the operation of organizational systems and the associated processing, storage, or transmission of
sensitive and/or critical data.
1.1.5. The risk assessment process results in the development of risk acceptance criteria, including the
definition of risk tolerance levels.
1.1.6. The risk assessment process prioritizes and documents risk treatment activities.
1.1.7. The risk assessment process results in the identification of residual risks and actions taken regarding
residual risk.
1.1.8. The risk assessment process includes monitoring procedures over risk treatment activities (refer to
section 1.3 below).
1.1.9. Risk assessment activities follow a risk management framework as a guideline for risk assessment
activities.
1.2. Security Risk Treatment
1.2.1. Information security management establishes a process for analyzing and categorizing the nature and
level of risks.
1.2.2. Risk treatment methods are established based on risk analysis and categorization and exclude risks to
be accepted or avoided based on risk tolerance criteria, as determined through the risk assessment process.
1.2.3. Risk treatment, avoidance, and acceptance decisions are documented.
1.3. Risk Monitoring and Reporting
1.3.1. Risk assessment procedures are performed on a periodic basis defined by management to assess
existing risks and to identify new risks.
1.3.2. Metrics for security risk management are defined, collected, analyzed, developed, and periodically
reported to management.
1.3.3. The information security management team plans, implements, and controls security risk
management processes and determines the frequency for monitoring and reporting on risks.

SISW Saas Vulnerability and Patch Management Policy requirements
1.1. Vulnerability and patch management standards must be established for each type of information resource.
1.1.1. The vulnerability and patch management process includes the following points of focus:
• Severity ratings and associated service level agreements (SLAs)
• External and internal vulnerability scanning criteria
• Vulnerability Analysis and Root Cause analysis
• Communication methods and frequencies
• Prioritization and response criteria
• Vulnerability remediation and patch implementation tracking mechanisms
• Vulnerability and patch management exceptions process
• Mechanisms to obtain vulnerability and patch data from applicable and trustworthy information resource

1.1.1.1. Security fix for components implemented must be obtained from a trustworthy source, for example:

1.1.2. Severity ratings and associated SLAs of vulnerabilities and patches must include the following categories:
• Critical (e.g., Easily exploitable by a remote unauthenticated attacker, Common Vulnerability Scoring System
(CVSS) score 9.0 and 10.0, 30 day remediation)
• High (e.g., Unauthenticated users can view data that should require authentication, local users can elevate
privilege, CVSS score between 7.0 and 8.9, 60 day remediation)
• Medium (e.g., Difficult to exploit, CVSS score between 4.0 and 6.9, 90 day remediation)
• Low (e.g., Minimal security impact, CVSS score between 3.9 and 0.1, 180 day remediation)
• Informational (e.g., information only, no security impact, CVSS score equal to 0.0, no remediation
commitment)

• Running discovery scans to identify new information resources at least weekly
• Performing scheduled scans for critical and non-critical information resources at least monthly and quarterly,

• Scanning information resources upon significant changes to the environment and on an ad-hoc basis, as
requested
• Timing scans according to patch release cycles of applicable vendor products
1.1.4. Vulnerabilities must be analyzed prior to implementing risk response measures to validate impact, risk
level, and appropriateness of response measure.
1.1.5. Periodically, root cause analyses and recurring vulnerability cause evaluation must be done.
1.1.6. The results of root cause analyses will be used to assess all other similar software and IT products to
proactively remediate any similar root causes.
1.1.7. At a minimum, prioritization and response criteria for vulnerability remediation and patch implementation

• Regulatory requirements

labelled per the SISW Saas Data Classification, Handling, and Sharing Policy. Such artifacts should be handled per
the labelled data classification level and sufficient confidentiality notices should be generated prior to sharing or
distribution.
1.7. Information security threat intelligence must be continually gathered, obtained, evaluated, and integrated
into the risk management process.
1.7.1. Contacts with external special interest groups, forums, or subscriptions identifying the latest threats and
vulnerabilities must be maintained.
1.7.2. SISW should determine the roles and responsibilities for receiving, analyzing, and prioritizing the
response to security threats and risk to the organization.
